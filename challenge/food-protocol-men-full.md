# THE FOOD PROTOCOL
## How to Eat for the Next 42 Days (Men)

---

## The Idea

We're not giving you a meal plan.

Meal plans work for 6 weeks and then you're back where you started, because nobody taught you how to eat. They handed you a list of someone else's meals to follow, and the day you ran out of recipes, you ran out of the program.

This doc teaches you the system. Three macros. A list of foods. A way to build a plate. A tracker on your phone. Run it for 42 days and you'll know how to eat for the rest of your life.

Three rules underneath everything:

1. **Don't bust your daily totals.** Going under is fine, especially the first two weeks. Going over breaks the protocol.
2. **Track every meal.** No exceptions.
3. **Two drinks per week max.** Zero is better if you can hold it.

The math will work. The discipline is on you.

---

## Step 1. Calculate Your Daily Macros

**Day 1 weigh-in.** Step on the scale first thing in the morning, after the bathroom, before food or coffee. Write that number down. That's your starting line.

**After that, weigh every Wednesday and Saturday morning** under the same conditions (first thing, post-bathroom, pre-food/coffee). Twice a week, same days, all 42 days. You can step on the scale every day if you want, but understand that daily readings will lie to you. Water, sodium, inflammation from a hard workout, and digestion can swing the number 2-4 lb overnight in either direction. Two readings a week, measured against each other over time, tell the truth. Daily readings make you crazy.

Then pick your **desired weight**. The weight you're trying to get to. If you have no clear goal, use the weight you felt best at, or pick a number 15-30 lb below where you are today. Don't overthink it. You can adjust later.

The protein formula runs off **desired weight**, not where you are today:

- **Protein:** desired weight × 1.0g, capped at **220g**
- **Carbs:** **220g** flat
- **Fat:** **65g** flat

Write these three numbers down. Put them on the fridge, the lock screen, the bathroom mirror, somewhere you'll see them. You don't need to recalculate every week.

### Why these numbers

**Protein scales with desired weight** because protein supports lean mass, not fat mass. If you're carrying an extra 40 lb of fat, that fat doesn't need more protein to maintain. Sizing protein to where you're going (not where you are) puts the right amount of food in front of you. Enough to preserve muscle, not so much that you're eating like a 230 lb athlete just to lose weight.

This is the difference between *losing weight* and *changing your body composition*. Lose protein, lose muscle, tank your metabolism. That's the trap most diets fall into.

**Carbs and fat stay flat** because hunger and energy needs don't scale linearly with bodyweight. Flat targets put everyone in a real deficit without leaving anyone crushed.

**The 220g protein cap exists** because past 220g, more protein doesn't add anything for most guys. It's also genuinely hard to eat that much meat day after day. Caps make the protocol *executable*.

### A Note on Volume

When you look at your target numbers, your first thought is probably: *that's a lot of food.* And it is, if you've been eating ultra-processed stuff your whole life. Clean food fills you up faster, weighs more on the plate, and forces your stomach to actually work.

**We don't expect you to hit your macros perfectly in the first week.** Your appetite has been calibrated to chips and soda, not chicken and rice. The first 10-14 days, you may fall short of your protein target. That's normal. As your body adapts and your metabolism comes back online, you'll be able to eat more of the right food without forcing it.

The hard rule in those early days: **only eat from the approved foods.** If you eat 60% of your target macros but every gram of it came from this list, you're winning. If you hit 100% of your macros with junk food, you've broken the protocol. Quality is non-negotiable. Quantity catches up.

## Step 2. Set Up Tracking (5 minutes)

You're going to track every meal for 42 days. Pick one tool, set it up today.

### Option A: Claude or ChatGPT (easiest, voice-first)

This is what we use. Total setup time: about 60 seconds.

**On day one, open Claude or ChatGPT, tap the mic, and read this aloud:**

> "You are my fitness tracker for the next 6 weeks. I'm doing a 42-day fat-loss challenge. My daily macro targets are [your protein]g protein, [your carbs]g carbs, and [your fat]g fat. Every time I tell you what I ate, estimate the macros for that meal and keep a running total for the day. When I ask, tell me how close I am to my targets and what I have left. If I'm short on protein with one meal to go, suggest a quick fix. Don't lecture me about food choices. Just track and tally. Reset the running total each morning. Ready?"

Fill in your three numbers where it says [your protein], [your carbs], [your fat].

After that, your job is simple. Every time you eat:

1. Open the app.
2. Tap the mic.
3. Say what you ate: *"I just ate 4 eggs, a cup of oatmeal, and 2 tablespoons of peanut butter."*
4. The AI estimates the macros and updates your running total.
5. Ask anytime: *"How am I doing on my targets so far today?"*

No food database, no barcode scanner, no logging app. You talk to your phone, your phone tells you the math.

### Option B: MyFitnessPal (for the spreadsheet types)

If you want it more structured:

1. Download MyFitnessPal (the free version is fine).
2. In settings, set custom macro goals. Enter your protein, carbs, and fat targets.
3. Log every meal. Use the barcode scanner on packaged stuff.
4. End of day, check the dashboard.

Both work. Pick the one you'll actually use.

The "I don't know how to track" excuse died the day voice mode launched. You can tell your phone what you ate while you walk to your car.

## Step 4. How to Build Your Plate

Every meal is the same shape:

> **1 PROTEIN + 1 CARB + 1 FAT**

That's it. Pull each from the lists above. Build 3-5 meals a day. Track everything.

### Per meal, aim for:

- **One protein serving** from the protein list above. Pick whatever fits the meal.
- **One or two carb servings** from the carb list. Bigger at breakfast and lunch, smaller at dinner.
- **One fat serving** from the fat list. Or let the protein carry the fat (eggs, salmon, peanut butter, avocado on toast). You don't need to add fat on purpose every meal.

### Rules of thumb

- **Protein anchors the meal.** Pick it first, build everything else around it.
- **Carbs go bigger at breakfast and lunch, smaller at dinner.** This is when you need them, to fuel your workout, your work, and your walk.
- **Fat usually rides along** with the protein (eggs, salmon, steak) or shows up as a finisher (oil on rice, butter on potato). You don't need to add fat on purpose at every meal. Let the protein do most of the work.
- **If a meal feels too small, add another carb serving.** Not another protein. Protein is sized to your daily target. You don't need more at any one meal.
- **Vegetables are a free-for-all.** They don't count against your macros for our purposes. Eat them whenever you want. They fill you up between meals.

The sample day below shows what this looks like in practice. Yours will look different. Different proteins, different carbs, your favorites. The shape stays the same.

## The Hunger Rule

You're going to be hungry sometimes. That's fat loss working. But there's hungry, and there's *starving*.

If you're starving past **day 4**, **post in the group.** We have options. Sometimes it's a carb bump, sometimes it's protein timing, sometimes it's a food swap that fills you up better. We work it out with you in the group, not solo. **Don't deviate on your own.**

The goal is fat loss you can sustain for **42 days**, not 4 days of suffering followed by a binge.

---

## What Doesn't Count

- **Alcohol past two drinks per week.** Two is the ceiling. Zero is the best answer if you can hold it. Past two drinks, the math stops working. Alcohol tanks recovery, kills sleep, drives water retention, and quietly adds 100-200 calories per drink your tracker can't fully see. Cutting alcohol way back does more for your sleep, skin, mood, and weight in 42 days than most of the food rules combined.
- **Liquid calories** (soda, juice, sweet tea, sweetened coffee drinks). Track them if you have them, but they're empty calories that don't fill you up. Better to spend those macros on real food.
- **"It's just a bite."** Track it. The whole point is learning what you actually eat. The bites add up. Pretending they don't is how you finish 42 days with nothing to show for it.

## Your Daily Routine

Five minutes of admin a day. The rest is just eating, training, and living.

1. **Weigh yourself every Wednesday and Saturday morning.** Same conditions every time. Write it down. Daily is allowed but the reading will lie to you (water, inflammation, digestion). Two days a week, compared over time, is what tells the truth.
2. **Plan tonight's meals roughly the night before.** What's defrosting, what's on the stove.
3. **Track every meal** in your app of choice.
4. **End of day check.** Did I hit my macros? Yes or no.
5. **Post your check-in to the group.** ✅ on macros + workout + 8,000 steps + reading + drink count for the week (out of 2).

## What This Will Do To You

If you run this clean for 42 days:

## Before You Start

We know this is a lot.

A lot of information. A lot of food. A lot of structure if you've been eating however you want for years. Just do the best you can.

If you can't hit every macro perfectly the first week, that's fine. If you can't eat all your protein on day three, that's fine. **The one rule that does not bend is this: only eat from the approved foods list.** Eat 60% of your numbers if that's where your appetite is, as long as every gram came from the list. Quality first. Quantity catches up.

Your metabolism has been calibrated to whatever you've been eating for the last ten years. It takes time to reset. Over the 42 days, your hunger will sync to real food, your stomach will handle more volume, and you'll stop feeling full halfway through your plate. This starts to feel less like a job. That's the body adapting. It happens to everyone who runs the protocol.

These are the steps you have to take to become the best version of yourself. There is no shortcut around them. But you are not running them alone.

The whole point of this challenge is the group. Jaden and Amber are in there every day. Other members are working the same problem you are. When you get stuck, when you don't know what to eat, when you're hungry, when life gets in the way, that's what the group is for. **We are here for you.** Post your question. Someone has been there. Someone has the answer. If they don't, we will figure it out together.

Show up. Eat the food. Stay in the group. Trust the math. Trust the people next to you.
