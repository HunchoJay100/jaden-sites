02 · The Problem We Solve

The cost of not knowing the process.

A 3,000 square foot custom home in Tennessee carries roughly $100,000 in general-contractor fee, and what that fee actually buys is process: which sub to call when, how to read a bid properly, how to sequence the trades, and what to look for at each inspection. It buys judgment, not labor. For most families, the gap between being able to write that check and not being able to is the difference between "could build" and "couldn't."

For the homeowner
Build alone, or don't build.
Skip the GC fee and you inherit every decision that GC was making behind the scenes. Without a playbook, the mistakes start to compound, with bad bids accepted, trades sequenced in the wrong order, and permits that come back rejected for things you didn't know to ask about. The overruns eat the savings, and sometimes they eat the whole project.
For the GC
Operating on memory.
Most working contractors run their jobs out of their head, with no written process to hand off to a project manager and no client-facing system to bring customers into. Margin leaks through disorganization week after week, and scaling beyond the operator is impossible without a system to actually install on top.
For the new builder
No on-ramp anywhere.
There's no formal training path for residential construction operators. Trade school teaches the trades, business school teaches business, and nobody teaches how to build a house the right way end to end. Most new entrants spend their first few builds making the same expensive mistakes everyone else has already made.

The market we're addressing.

  • There are roughly 1.4 million single-family housing starts a year in the US.
  • There are roughly 700,000 licensed residential GCs nationally, and most of them run on improvisation, which makes willingness to pay for a system that actually works unusually high.
  • The homeowner segment is the one growing fastest, as rising material costs, GC capacity constraints, and a younger demographic comfortable buying digital products all converge at the same time.
The thesis. The right process is the missing piece, and we package it once and sell it to everyone who needs it. The methodology underneath stays the same across all three audiences; only the story on top changes depending on who's listening.

03 · The Three Audiences

One methodology. Three stories.

The same Order of Operations works for all three audiences, the same portal serves them, and the same articles read for all of them. We segment by language and onboarding rather than by product, which means each new audience expansion adds funnel without adding engineering debt underneath.

Audience 01
Self-contracting homeowners
Our original audience, building once in their life on a project worth $400K and up, with no GC fee in the budget. They tend to be educated and internet-native, and they're willing to do the work themselves as long as someone hands them a playbook that actually works.
  • Story: Save the GC fee, keep the equity.
  • Tier fit: Blueprint & Navigator
  • Volume signal: ~140K starts/yr at 10% of total US
Audience 02
General contractors
Working operators running anywhere from 2 to 15 active builds at once. They have the highest willingness to pay of the three groups, and they aren't buying to learn the trade so much as to install a system on top of what they already know. Natural Tier 3 customers and the right fit for the recurring community layer when it ships.
  • Story: Run your builds like a $50M shop.
  • Tier fit: The Operator
  • Volume signal: ~700K active US GCs
Audience 03
Aspiring builders
Brand-new entrants who want to build, either for themselves once or as a business going forward. The largest of the three audiences by a wide margin and the slowest-converting one, but also the biggest recurring-revenue prize once the cohort and certification layers ship in Q4.
  • Story: Learn to build the right way from day one.
  • Tier fit: The Operator (or Blueprint into cohort)
  • Volume signal: indeterminate, but largest of the three

Why it matters.

A single-audience play caps the funnel at a few hundred thousand customers a year, which is fine but not generational. The three-audience play, running against the same product spine, opens the addressable market into the millions. The story shifts depending on who's listening, but the portal underneath doesn't change, and that's where the real operating leverage lives.

05 · The Methodology (cont.)

Built inside an active construction team.

This isn't theory work. Every article, every deliverable, and every step in the Order of Operations has been pressure-tested on active Flynn Development jobsites. That field-testing is the moat, and it's the one thing nobody starting from scratch can replicate quickly.

Provenance.

ConsultMD is the published methodology of Flynn Development, the residential construction team Jaden and Jason Flynn run. We've more than doubled the business every year for the past four years, and we have eight figures in sales already booked for 2026. The methodology inside ConsultMD is the same playbook running underneath that growth.

Every article in the library was sharpened on a real build, every deliverable was written to solve a real problem the team kept running into, and the Order of Operations itself was reverse-engineered from how Flynn Development actually runs jobs at scale, then productized into something teachable.

Legal posture.

The product is positioned as education, methodology, and toolkit, which means it isn't legal, financial, or construction advice. Every article carries the appropriate disclaimer making that clear, and every customer-facing deliverable (work orders, lien waivers, COIs) ships unbranded so it travels properly between homeowner and sub without putting our name on a document we don't control downstream.

The budget tracker is tracking-only, recording estimated, actual, and variance side by side. We never originate a number for the customer, so there's zero liability exposure on the accuracy of any estimate.

Design system & quality.

The methodology ships against a documented design system: Plus Jakarta Sans display paired with DM Sans body throughout, a light theme for customer-facing fillable forms (so they feel neutral when they land in a sub's inbox), and a darker branded treatment for the educational content. Articles render through a Puppeteer pipeline straight from HTML, and spreadsheets ship from Python with live formulas and conditional formatting baked in. Every output is built to the polish you'd expect from a product that prices in at $25K.

The Flynn Development connection isn't a marketing line. It's the actual reason the methodology works. Construction teams teaching from active jobsites publish a different product than agencies repackaging public knowledge into ebooks, and that difference shows up in every artifact we ship.

08 · The Operator Side

Built to scale.

The bottleneck for any consulting business is the number of people you have managing clients. The Operator Platform is the admin side of ConsultMD, a multi-consultant command center designed to scale to hundreds of active clients at once without losing the white-glove feel that makes the product worth paying for in the first place.

Three-role architecture.

  • Super Admin (Jaden), with full visibility across every client, every consultant, and every metric in the system.
  • Consultants, scoped to their assigned subset of clients with row-level security enforced at the database level, so a consultant literally cannot see clients outside their book. Adding a new consultant takes about thirty seconds.
  • Clients, who have an unchanged customer experience and never see the admin layer at all.

The health-score engine.

Every active client carries a computed health score from 0 to 100, recalculated on read so it always reflects current reality. Penalties stack across SLA breaches, stalled builds, low call usage, budget overruns, and prolonged inactivity. The operator dashboard surfaces attention-needed clients automatically, so clients stop falling through the cracks between consultant check-ins.
