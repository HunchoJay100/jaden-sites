01 / SITUATION

What changed after the second walkthrough.

The V6 Plan

Full conversion to conditioned living space.

Framed walls, traditional windows, insulation, drywall, code-compliant electrical, and an HVAC branch extended from the house. Beautiful. Permit-heavy. $60K.

Her Real Concern

Winter rain comes in sideways.

Grace doesn't need a heated office. She needs the porch to stop soaking through on wet Tennessee winters. A screen porch alone won't cut it. Full enclosure is more than the problem requires.

The Alternative

Operable vinyl over existing openings.

The structure in A01–A04 is already there: roof, posts, knee walls, roof line. Fill the openings with a 4-track system and she has screens in summer, sealed vinyl in winter, one product, one season-line.

The 4-Track System

How 4-track works.

Four independent tracks. Two screen panels, two vinyl panels, all sliding vertically within one aluminum frame. Grace reconfigures the opening by hand in three seconds. No motors, no controller, no subscription — this is the side-wall workhorse in the hybrid, and the full-porch product in Path A.

PGT Eze-Breeze · 4-Track Vertical Vinyl & Screen

The whole porch gets the same panel system. Always-on rain protection. One product, one install, the lowest-risk path to a usable winter porch.

Eze-Breeze is the industry-standard 4-track system from PGT, the largest vinyl-window manufacturer in the U.S. Each opening gets four sliding panels — two screen, two clear vinyl — that Grace reconfigures by hand. Slide vinyl down in winter and the porch is watertight. Slide it up in summer and she's on a screened porch.

For Grace's opening plan, we fill thirteen bays: four 4×6 main bays and four transoms across the front, four bays on the left, and the small 2×6 bay on the right. Existing exterior doors stay in place, both getting storm-door upgrades.

The hybrid.

Phantom Executive Motorized · Eze-Breeze 4-Track · Side-by-Side

Motorized Phantom clear-vinyl screens disappear into the ceiling on the front wall. Eze-Breeze 4-track panels handle the side walls. Grace gets the showstopper where it matters and the workhorse everywhere else.

The front wall is what she sees every morning from her desk. At the touch of a button, the Phantom motorized system rolls down from a hidden housing — clear vinyl for rain, screen mesh for bugs — and disappears back into the ceiling when the weather clears. Porch reads as wide open on good days, fully enclosed on bad ones.

The side walls get Eze-Breeze 4-track panels. Same manual sliders as Path A — bulletproof, always there, no motors to service. Best of both worlds: high-drama front, no-drama sides.

Fixed screen + roll-down curtains.

Screeneze System + Clear Marine Vinyl Drops

The most economical approach. Fixed premium screens year-round, with clear marine vinyl roll-down curtains that Grace deploys manually on the front when weather rolls in.

Screeneze is a taut-for-life aluminum-and-vinyl frame system with no spline — it won't sag or need re-stretching. The drop curtains are UV-treated 20-gauge clear marine vinyl with weighted hems and stayput fasteners. Not as polished as the vinyl-panel paths, but absolutely functional.

This is the "solve the rain problem and stop" option. If Grace eventually wants to upgrade, the Screeneze stays in place and the curtains come down.

The front wall is the moment — the view, the entertainment, the reason the porch exists. Phantom makes it theater. The side walls don't need to be, and Eze-Breeze does that job for a third of the price. One product where it matters, the other where it doesn't. Still $26,000 under the V6 plan, and none of the permit or panel-upsize triggers kick in.

Before we re-quote

  4. Pull Eze-Breeze dealer quote for the nine side-wall openings.
