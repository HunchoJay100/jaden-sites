Guard the well.

Proverbs 4 · Keep Your Heart

Chapter four opens with a father talking to his son. Get wisdom. Hold onto it. Do not let go. The warning is about what happens when a man stops guarding what is coming into his life.

The room landed on the line, "keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life." We talked about what a man is actually letting through the gate in his day. What he watches. What he scrolls. What he tolerates in his own head. A polluted source poisons everything downstream.

Takeaways
  • The heart is the well. Everything else drinks from it.
  • A man is responsible for what he lets through the gate.
  • Small inputs over time become the whole direction of a life.
The Three Questions
  1. What are you letting into your life right now that you would not let into your son's life?
  2. Where has wisdom been trying to get your attention and you have kept walking?
  3. Name one gate you need to start guarding this week.
"Keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life."

Trust before you understand.

Proverbs 3 · Trust in the Lord

Proverbs 3 is one of the chapters most men know a verse from without ever having read the whole thing. We read it together and what kept coming back around the room was the lines about honoring God with your first, not your leftovers.

What it actually looks like to trust when the numbers do not make sense yet. The men who had the most to say were the ones who had been burned by their own plan.

Takeaways
  • Trust is a decision you make before you see the outcome.
  • Your first offering tells the truth about who you trust.
  • God promises direction, not a map. There is a difference.
The Three Questions
  1. Where is your plan working, and where is it breaking?
  2. What would change if you gave God the first and not the last?
  3. What is one step forward that requires trust this week?
"Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding."

Wisdom is loud.

Proverbs 1 · Wisdom Calls Aloud

First chapter. Solomon does not open quietly. Wisdom is personified as a woman standing in the public square yelling at men who keep walking past her. The question that hung in the room was simple. How often are we the guys walking past?

Wisdom usually shows up long before the consequence. In a gut feeling. In a mentor's warning. In scripture you already read. The ignoring is the part that costs.

Takeaways
  • Wisdom is not hiding. It is usually already speaking.
  • Fools are not dumb. They are just men who will not listen.
  • The fear of the Lord is where real knowledge starts.
The Three Questions
  1. Who has been telling you something true that you keep dismissing?
  2. Where is a gut check you have been ignoring?
  3. What does "the fear of the Lord" look like in your Monday?
"The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge; fools despise wisdom and instruction."
